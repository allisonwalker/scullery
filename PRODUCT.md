# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small households — couples, friends, flatmates — who plan meals together and share a kitchen. They cook regularly but are not chefs: they want to be organised without effort. Primary user is a household manager (typically one person who initiates planning) with one or more collaborators who join via invite. Context: phone or laptop at home, usually in the evening before a grocery run. Not in a hurry — this is a ritual, not a task.

## Product Purpose

Scullery is a shared household meal planner. It helps households decide what to eat each week, keep a recipe library, and generate consolidated grocery lists — with an AI layer that suggests recipes and adapts to preferences. Success looks like: a household that plans ahead without dread, wastes less food, and shops once.

## Brand Personality

Rustic, joyful, unhurried. The name "scullery" is deliberately old-fashioned — a working part of the house, unpretentious, warm. The voice is the capable friend who loves to cook: knowledgeable but never intimidating, practical but with pleasure. Design should feel like a well-used kitchen notebook, not a productivity dashboard.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate SaaS (Notion, Linear)**: flat greys, cold precision, zero warmth — the opposite of a kitchen.
- **Food delivery apps (Uber Eats, DoorDash)**: garish promotional energy, deal-first hierarchy, no relationship with food.
- **Diet / calorie trackers (MyFitnessPal)**: measurement-obsessed, clinical, joyless — food as numbers.
- **Generic AI tools**: beige backgrounds, sparkles everywhere, system sans-serif, the full 2026 AI monoculture stack.

## Design Principles

1. **The kitchen over the dashboard.** Every surface should feel domestic and handmade, not engineered. Prefer warmth and texture over clinical precision.
2. **Calm by default, smart on demand.** The weekly plan is the anchor. AI features assist — they never lead. Keep the interface quiet until the user asks for help.
3. **Shared without ceremony.** The app is used by multiple people in a household. Anything that works for one person should work for two, without extra steps or handoffs.
4. **Unhurried rhythm.** Meal planning is a weekly ritual, not a sprint. Interactions should feel deliberate, not urgent. Avoid counting animations, streaks, and metrics.
5. **Honest about what it knows.** If the AI suggests something, say so. If a grocery list is incomplete, show it. Never fake confidence.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline. Reduced-motion support required. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning. Touch targets ≥ 44×44pt (the app is used on mobile while shopping).
